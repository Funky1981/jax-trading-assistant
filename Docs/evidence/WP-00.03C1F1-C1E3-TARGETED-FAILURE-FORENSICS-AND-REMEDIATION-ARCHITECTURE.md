# WP-00.03C1F1 — C1E3 targeted failure forensics and remediation architecture

Date: 2026-08-19
Status: design complete; Phase 00 remains **NO-GO**; no remediation implemented

## Decision

Architecture disposition: **A. V5 architecture is fundamentally sound; narrow remediation is sufficient.**

The v5 contract can express every frozen C1E3 answer. Its principal residual problems are not a missing causal graph or a Luna capability ceiling. They are: (1) evaluation treats surface-form issuer aliases as different semantic issuers even when the resolver proves exact one-to-one identity; (2) the prompt does not state the negative boundary for principal proxy candidacy sharply enough; (3) the prompt does not explicitly forbid public bodies, indexes, commodities, sectors, markets, groups, products, and ambiguous common nouns from `issuer_attributions`; (4) a small number of role reversals remain; and (5) the validator's rule that every `UNRESOLVED` result must have LOW `mapping_confidence` creates contract retries without adding causal information.

The minimum direction is therefore a versioned prompt clarification plus a versioned deterministic semantic-identity comparator and a narrow validator revision. Keep the v5 wire schema, C1E policy, and asset resolver behavior unchanged. The comparator may use exact, collision-aware issuer aliases and legal-suffix normalization but must never infer parent/subsidiary/brand relationships, choose securities, or use fuzzy similarity. The future scorer must compare typed issuer identities through that component while preserving the immutable C1E3 score.

Sixty-one cases contain at least one legal-name/surface-alias mismatch; 51 of the 70 cases in the score's all-case failure inventory have no substantive error beyond that identity form. ### A. Exact issuer identity

The dominant failure begins at the contract/evaluation boundary. EventInput normally says the receipt-time surface form (`Tesla`, `IBM`, `Google`), v5 asks Luna to copy the exact principal issuer name between its own fields, and frozen typed labels use legal names. Nothing in the provider input supplies legal canonical names. Luna therefore did the safe thing in most cases: retained the observed issuer surface form. The validator correctly enforced internal equality, the C1E policy preserved it, and the resolver correctly mapped supported aliases.

This is not evidence for asking Luna to invent legal suffixes, parent names, or tickers. Nor is it a resolver failure. The missing piece is a deterministic semantic-identity comparison that is exact and collision-aware. Same-name ambiguity must remain ambiguity; brand, division, parent, and subsidiary identities must never be collapsed without an explicit frozen alias relationship. Unsupported issuer identity and asset support remain separate.

Origin attribution: EventInput supplies surface identity; prompt is silent about legal-form expectations; schema free text is sufficient; Luna mostly preserves input; validator and C1E policy are neutral; scorer exactness creates the nominal failures; resolver behaves correctly.

### B. Entity versus exposure

V5 already has separate containers: `issuer_attributions` for traded issuers and `principal_proxy_candidates` for bounded exposures. Correct labels for every observed case fit those containers. The missing prompt rule is explicit negative typing. Luna placed semiconductor stocks/oil market and gold market/S&P 500 into EQUAL_PRINCIPAL issuer records, and made semiconductor stocks or gold market a PRINCIPAL issuer. It also emitted the Federal Reserve and S&P 500 as contextual issuers and treated crop `Apple` as contextual issuer text.

This is mostly prompt usage, not a missing schema union. Adding an `identity_class` field would ask Luna to repeat a distinction it already failed to obey and add contract burden. A narrow deterministic checker may reject an issuer string that exactly matches a frozen bounded-exposure identity, but must not parse EventInput or prose and must not convert arbitrary text into a proxy.

### C. False principal proxy

Seven candidates were false: Generalization selected `SP500_NAMED_INDEX`, `US_RATES_CATEGORY`, `US_RATES_CATEGORY`, and `SEMICONDUCTOR_GROUP`; Boundary selected `US_RATES_CATEGORY`, `US_RATES_CATEGORY`, and `SEMICONDUCTOR_GROUP`. They were historical calibration, commentator affiliation, speculative beneficiary, proposed indirect benefit, infrastructure commentary, inflation commentary, or an unallocated supplier warning. The reasons often said the proxy was weak, approximate, absent, or did not cleanly represent the event.

The prompt says a proxy is not a fallback for direct-issuer ambiguity, but does not say it is also not a fallback for *no issuer* or for the nearest allowlisted topic. The general repair is: a candidate exists only when the bounded exposure itself is the principal subject; absence of a direct issuer, keyword association, secondary costs/benefits, commentary, or lack of a better enum is insufficient. Policy accepted all seven because a singleton candidate is the only evidence it is allowed to read. No policy rule can distinguish these false candidates from the 9 correct singleton candidates using current typed state alone.

### D. Causal-role reversal

Five cases had real role errors. Apple, explicitly unaffected by Corning's furnace issue, was SECONDARY_AFFECTED rather than CONTEXT_ONLY. A speculative Humana example was SECONDARY_AFFECTED rather than CONTEXT_ONLY. A historical Nike exhibit made Nike PRINCIPAL rather than CONTEXT_ONLY. In the Rivian/NVIDIA supply-chain case Luna made the upstream supplier the principal and the issuer whose schedule slipped secondary—the model followed causal mechanism rather than the classified event subject. In the unallocated Tesla/Ford warning Luna used SECONDARY_AFFECTED rather than POSSIBLE_PRINCIPAL.

The enum is expressive enough. The prompt needs operational tests: `SECONDARY_AFFECTED` requires a genuine evidenced effect; explicit no-impact and conjectural examples are CONTEXT_ONLY. `PRINCIPAL` is the issuer whose event/condition is being classified, not automatically the upstream mechanism, speaker, or historical subject. `POSSIBLE_PRINCIPAL` applies when one of named issuers may own the principal effect but receipt-time evidence cannot choose.

### E. Completeness

Exact completeness was 15/80 because legal-name forms differed. After unambiguous identity equivalence it is 71/80. The nine substantive incomplete cases are role/candidate errors, not a general omission epidemic. Equal issuer causality was retained in all expected equal-principal cases. Most completeness loss is therefore an identity-comparison contract issue; the residual is instruction-following on role/exposure typing.

### F. Equal and possible principal

EQUAL_PRINCIPAL worked for all seven expected equal-issuer cases modulo aliases, and Luna never arbitrarily selected one of those issuers. It failed only when equal *exposures* were typed as issuers. POSSIBLE_PRINCIPAL was correct in two expected ambiguity cases, missed once for two issuers, and overused once for an ambiguous common noun. Refinement is narrow: possible principal requires evidence that the token denotes a traded issuer and that it could own the principal event, not merely uncertainty about what a word means.

## Prompt adequacy

| Pattern | V5 status | General remediation rule |
|---|---|---|
| Legal name versus surface alias | Missing expectation and impossible when legal identity is absent | Preserve the narrowest issuer identity established by receipt-time evidence; never invent suffix, parent, subsidiary, ticker, or legal relationship. Deterministic identity comparison handles approved aliases. |
| Proxy as nearest fallback | Ambiguous/missing negative rule | Candidate only when that exact bounded exposure is principal. No issuer, keyword, commentary, secondary effect, or nearest enum is insufficient. |
| Exposure/non-issuer in issuer array | Implicit but not explicit enough | Enumerate prohibited issuer kinds generically; route principal bounded exposures only to candidates. |
| CONTEXT_ONLY vs SECONDARY_AFFECTED | Definitions present, test missing | Secondary requires an evidenced benefit/harm; hypothetical, no-impact, speaker, comparator, and historical mentions are context. |
| Event subject vs upstream mechanism | Terminology too abstract | Principal is the issuer whose reported event/condition is classified, not automatically its upstream cause. |
| POSSIBLE_PRINCIPAL | Definition incomplete | Token must first be evidenced as a traded issuer; ambiguity must concern ownership of the principal event. |
| Equal issuers | Adequate and followed | Preserve. |
| Mapping confidence on abstention | Prompt does not explain validator coupling | Remove coupling; do not spend prompt tokens teaching a non-causal invariant. |

## Contract adequacy

Classification: **A. V5 representation is sufficient; failures are primarily prompt/model use plus identity comparison.** Every frozen correct answer is representable by the existing five roles, issuer array, proxy candidate array, and mapping projection. The typed v2 sidecars demonstrate this directly. No observed case requires parent/legal-issuer fields, an entity/exposure union, evidence spans, role confidence, or a causal graph.

Do not add `identity_class`: the containers already type issuer versus exposure, and another model-authored enum cannot prove identity. Do not add parent/subsidiary fields without evidence of an unrepresentable case. Keep historical v5 evidence readable unchanged.

## Policy, identity, and proxy architecture

Policy adequacy: **remain unchanged**. It receives enough typed evidence when Luna supplies it correctly, does not parse prose, and caused no raw-correct→wrong transitions.

Issuer identity: retain free-text issuer plus a deterministic, versioned semantic identity normalizer used for comparison only. It should perform case/punctuation/Unicode normalization, exact collision-aware corporate-suffix equivalence, and explicitly frozen one-to-one aliases. Ambiguous collisions return non-equivalent/ambiguous. It must not use fuzzy matching, EventInput prose, tickers, market data, parent inference, or resolver coverage. The unchanged asset resolver then maps a selected semantic issuer to supported assets. This preserves Luna-understands / resolver-maps separation.

Proxy architecture: `principal_proxy_candidates` is fundamentally sound. It achieved 69/80 exact candidate-set correctness and represented every adjudicated answer. The failure is over-selection (7 false candidates) and exposure/issuer confusion (4 cases), not lack of capacity. Retain it; clarify principal-exposure semantics. Do not remove model-selected candidates because determining whether an exposure is principal is semantic work. Do not let policy accept a candidate based on reason prose.
